## Verdict

**`fail`, recorded as `revise-before-implementation`.**

The rule is right in kind and should land, but its wording should be revised before the pre-registered run, because the run tests the wording.\
Three of its sentences draw a true lesson's boundary in the wrong place, the overgeneralised-lesson defect.

1. "Can neither confirm a change nor catch one" is false for damage: a probe readers pass without the document still shows a new version that misleads them, and "decide nothing" discards the probe in exactly that case.
2. "Readers holding the document must do measurably better" names no version and one direction: the earlier-record route then discards every probe of a concern only the new version addresses, and a probe on which a version misleads readers below those holding nothing is dropped, though the bolded rule, "tell ... from", is two-sided.
3. "Measurably better" states no margin, and the corpus has already read it two ways in one day: `0104` required a gap of 0.3 with Fisher p below 0.05, and `0106`'s screen a gap of 0.3 alone.

Measured on a synthetic run record put through the pre-registered predicates, a new version that drops a control probe from 12 of 12 cells to 4 of 12, while readers holding nothing stay at 12 of 12, is sent "to the director for ratification".\
The same record put through the predicates proposed under *Deltas* is refuted.

The origin is one incident, `B88`, but the core is not an overgeneralisation: the same check was applied in `0104` and `0105` and changed the outcome, since the new `A4` trap replaced the old one only after it separated.

## Tensions

1. **New rule 4 against rule 3, control probes.**
   Rule 3 says control probes are what lets the test fail the new version.
   Rule 4's "decide nothing" removes a control probe readers pass without the document even when the new version damages it, weakening the one capability rule 3 exists for.
   Resolution: a probe that does not separate counts for nothing in the change's favour and still counts against it.
2. **New rule 4 against rule 6, grounded answers.**
   Rule 6 scores a grounded answer above an ungrounded one, and readers holding nothing can never ground an answer.
   Under rule 6's scoring every probe separates by construction, so rule 4 never bites on a comprehension test.
   The test of record avoids this by scoring content only, which departs from rule 6 without saying so.
   Resolution: rule 4 judges separation on what readers decide, not on whether they cite the document.
3. **New rule 4 against rule 5, readers holding only the document.**
   No conflict: readers holding nothing are a separate group, and "only" excludes anything more, not nothing.
   Rule 4 uses readers before rule 5 defines them, which is acceptable because rule 4 qualifies rule 3.
4. **New rule 4 against *When to apply*.**
   No conflict: the *Not for* bullet decides whether any test runs, and rule 4 adds a group of readers only to a test that already runs, and only where no earlier record holds the comparison.
   It is not the ceremony the bullet warns of, because it names the failure it guards against and can be pruned when that failure becomes impossible.
5. **New rule 4 against *Why this terminates*.**
   No conflict on the regress: execution still ends it, and rule 4 says what a cold reader's correct answer is evidence of.
   But the section says every rule this corpus can mechanise, it does, and rule 4 is mechanisable and not mechanised.
6. **New rule 4 against `tools/eval.mjs compare`.**
   The tool exits 1 if any probe's head mean falls below base, whether the probe separates or not.
   "Decide nothing" contradicts it; the proposed wording agrees with it.
7. **New rule 4 against the method's own *Good - reasoning* example.**
   The example runs readers of the old and new versions only, and says "The controls score identically".
   An author working from that exemplar, as `PC1` directs, skips rule 4.
8. **`A8` binary gate against small samples.**
   At 12 cells per arm a gap screen without a significance test is what a run can afford.
   The rule should leave the margin to the pre-registration rather than imply a precision it lacks.

## Deltas

**D1 - replace rule 4.**

Replace the five lines of rule 4 on the branch with:
```
4. **Let a probe judge a change only if it can tell readers holding the document from readers holding none.**
   Readers holding either version must do better on it than readers holding nothing, or worse, by a margin stated before the run; a probe that capable readers pass, or fail, whatever they hold measures the reader, not the document, and cannot show an improvement.
   Check this in an earlier run's record before spending a new run, where that record already gave the same probe to readers holding the content it tests and to readers holding nothing; otherwise give readers holding nothing the same probes in the run that judges the change.
   A probe that does not separate them counts for nothing in the change's favour, and still counts against it if readers holding the new version do worse on it than readers holding the old.
   Judge separation on what readers decide, not on whether they cite the document, which readers holding nothing cannot do.
   Control probes need this as much as the rest.
   A trap - a task that shows whether an agent acts on guidance, not what it can answer - is held to this rule too.
```

| Sentence | Reason |
|---|---|
| Readers holding either version must do better on it than readers holding nothing, or worse, by a margin stated before the run; ... and cannot show an improvement. | Two-sided like the bolded rule; the margin is left to the pre-registration because `0104` and `0106` read "measurably" two ways; "cannot show an improvement" replaces a claim false for damage. |
| Check this in an earlier run's record before spending a new run, where that record already gave the same probe to readers holding the content it tests and to readers holding nothing; ... | A record holding only the old version cannot show a probe of what the change adds, and can show a trap whose guidance a trigger change leaves as it was, which was `B88`'s case. |
| A probe that does not separate them counts for nothing in the change's favour, and still counts against it if readers holding the new version do worse on it than readers holding the old. | Keeps rule 3's purpose, and agrees with `tools/eval.mjs compare`. |
| Judge separation on what readers decide, not on whether they cite the document, which readers holding nothing cannot do. | Without it, rule 6's scoring makes every comprehension probe separate by construction. |
| A trap ... is held to this rule too. | "Is a probe in this sense" lets a reader extend rules 5 to 9 to traps. |

**D2 - repair the *Good - reasoning* example.**\
Replace the line `> Three fresh readers get the old charter and three the new, blind,` with `> Three fresh readers get the old charter, three the new and three nothing, blind,`.\
Replace `The controls score identically.` with `The controls score identically on both versions, and above the readers given nothing.`.

**D3 - bring `0106` and its test into line.**\
Restate the *Each changed sentence* table for D1 and D2.\

**D4 - correct the cell count.**\
Correct "spent 96 cells on a trap" in `0106` and in `B94` to "spent 24 of its 96 cells on a trap", under a correction banner, not silently.

## Implementation guardrails

1. Nothing lands on `main` before the pre-registered run decides, as `0106` states.
2. `m2-head` holds a pinned revision carrying the final wording, and the predicates script refuses a run where it does not.
3. A control probe the new version damages counts against the change whether or not it separates.
4. Separation is judged on content, and judges are told that labelling a claim as inferred never raises or lowers a score.
5. Frozen records keep the old rule numbers (`RU2`); live citations are searched again on the landing commit.
6. The commit carries no tool attribution and one concern.
7. `tools/s6-one-sentence-per-line.mjs`, `tools/s13-plain-ascii.sh` and `tools/check-all.sh` pass on the landing commit.
